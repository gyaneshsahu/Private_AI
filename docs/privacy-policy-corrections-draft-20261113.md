### 2.1 Why we may use your data (legal basis)

> We use your data only for the purposes below. For each purpose we say what we use, the legal basis in the GDPR, how long we keep it, and who else receives it.
>
> **Your account and sign-in.** We keep your email address, the fact that you sign in with Google, and the number Google gives your Google account. If you add a passkey, we keep its public key and device type. We keep your sign-in sessions and security notices about your account. We do not keep your name or picture from Google, and we do not keep Google's access keys for your account. We do not store your IP address or browser type with your sessions. During the alpha we also check that your email address is on our invitation list.
> Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR, because we need this to give you the account you asked for.
> How long: until your account is erased. A sign-in session ends after one hour without use, or when you sign out.
> Who else receives it: Google, when you sign in on Google's page; Google is responsible for its own part. Render, our hosting provider, runs our server and database.
>
> **Chat answers, request records and your allowance.** Your sealed question passes through our server to the AI provider named in "Who processes your messages". We cannot open it. For each request we record your account, the time, the request state and the model. [Tinfoil only: the token counts and the cost at published rates.] We use these records to count your allowance, to keep to our spending limit, and to let only one request run at a time.
> Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR to answer you and to count your allowance. Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR for our legitimate interest in controlling costs and stopping misuse.
> How long: 90 days, or until your current allowance period ends if that is later. A daily job then deletes them.
> Who else receives it: the AI provider receives the sealed question. Render stores the request records.
>
> **Web search.** [Show only while web search is switched on.] A search happens only when you approve the exact search text, or when you have switched on automatic search. We use the search text after recognised identifiers were removed on your device, and the address of the result page our server opens. Our server keeps a short approval record for at most 5 minutes. It holds a coded fingerprint of the approved text, not the text itself, and it is deleted when used.
> Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR, because you ask for the search. [Lawyer: the Art. 30 draft says Art. 6(1)(a); choose one and use it in both.]
> How long: we do not save the search text, results or page text on our server. Brave keeps the search text under its own policy, for up to 90 days.
> Who else receives it: Brave Software, Inc., USA (Brave Search). This is a transfer outside the EU. [Transfer basis: lawyer to confirm.] The website we open sees our server's address, not yours.
>
> **Security and protection against misuse.** To limit repeated sign-in attempts, our server keeps your IP address together with the sign-in step for a short time. Old entries are deleted as new sign-in attempts arrive, usually after about one minute. Our own service logs hold only counts and status lines. They hold no content, no account and no IP address. Our hosting provider Render, and Cloudflare acting for Render, see the IP address of each request to our server.
> Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, for our legitimate interest in keeping the service secure and available. You can object; see "Your rights and how to use them".
> How long: our rate-limit entries, as above. Render's network records: under Render's terms [retention: owner to find out].
>
> **Health and other sensitive details.** [Show only while the consent step is switched on.] If you write about your health, or about religion, sexual life, ethnic origin or other sensitive matters, those details are part of your message. Recognised identifiers are replaced on your device, but health details are not. We process these details only if you agree.
> Legal basis: Art. 9(2)(a) GDPR (your explicit consent), together with Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.
> You can withdraw your consent at any time in Settings, Privacy & data. Withdrawal does not make earlier processing unlawful. If you say no, you can still use CometRow; please leave such details out of your messages. Your choice is stored only in your browser (see "What is stored in your browser"). We do not keep a copy on our server.
>
> **Your requests to us.** When you write to us, we use your email address and your message to answer you. When we erase an account, we keep a record that the erasure happened, with no email address.
> Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR together with Art. 12 to 22 GDPR, for requests about your rights. Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR for other support questions.
> How long: [owner to decide, for example until the request is closed plus three years].
> Who else receives it: our email provider for these addresses [owner to name].
>
> **Billing.** Paid plans are not available yet, and we process no payment data. Before paid plans start, this policy will say what we process for billing, on which legal basis, and how long tax law requires us to keep invoices.

### 2.2 Your rights and how to use them

> You have these rights under the GDPR. Using them is free of charge.
>
> - **Access (Art. 15).** You can download the records we hold about your account from the account page at any time. You can also write to us and ask.
> - **Correction (Art. 16).** If something we hold is wrong, write to us. Your email address comes from your Google account; changing it in the app is not available.
> - **Erasure (Art. 17).** Closing your account in the app is not available yet, because it needs an email code and email is switched off. Until it works, write to [support address] from the email address of your account and ask us to erase it. We first stop access to the account. Then we erase your account, your sign-in methods and sessions, your security notices, your allowance and your request records. We confirm when it is done. Some things stay: the record that the erasure happened (an account number that is not linked to you any more, and dates, with no email address); copies in our hosting provider's database backups until they expire [backup window, owner to confirm]; and the AI provider's records, which are not linked to your account. Your saved conversations are only on your devices. Delete them with "Delete everything on this device" in Saved, on each device. If your address is still on the invitation list, signing in again later starts a new, empty account.
> - **Restriction (Art. 18).** You can ask us to limit how we use your data, for example while we check a correction.
> - **Data portability (Art. 20).** The download on the account page is a machine-readable file (JSON). Your saved conversations are in your encrypted backup files.
> - **Objection (Art. 21).** Where we rely on our legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f)), you can object for reasons from your particular situation. We then stop, unless we have compelling legitimate grounds or need the data for legal claims.
> - **Withdrawing consent (Art. 7(3)).** You can withdraw consent at any time, with effect for the future. Health details: change your choice in Settings, Privacy & data. Automatic search: switch it off in the app.
> - **Automated decisions (Art. 22).** We make no decisions about you by automated means that have legal effects for you or affect you in a similarly significant way.
>
> **How to use your rights.** Write to [privacy address], or to [support address] for erasure, from the email address of your account. We do not ask for identity documents. We answer without undue delay and within one month. If a request is complex, we may take two more months; we tell you within the first month if so.
>
> **Your right to complain (Art. 77).** You can complain to a data protection authority. The authority for us is [Land authority]. You can also complain to the authority in the EU country where you live or work, or where you think the problem happened.

`[Land authority]`: the repo already names "the Landesbeauftragte für Datenschutz und Informationsfreiheit Nordrhein-Westfalen (LDI NRW), Düsseldorf" (`deployment/legal-identity.json`, `supervisoryAuthorityNote`). Use that field here, so the name lives in one place.
