# German and English interface (13 November 2026)

Owner requirement of 13 November: a German and English interface. The browser language decides (de gives German, anything else English), there is a visible DE/EN switch kept only on the device, and never IP or email. All user-facing text is translated. The German legal texts are binding. A change in one language must flag the other.

**Status:** built, merged on `codex/development-handoff`, CI green. Not deployed. The German legal texts are drafts that need your approval and a legal read **before the German interface goes live** (see "Before you deploy German").

## What was built

| Part | What it does | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Language layer | Browser language on the first visit, then your pick; the switch saves it in this browser only; `<html lang>` follows at once | `src/i18n.tsx`, `src/text/core.ts` |
| DE/EN switch | In the conversation header, in Settings (Appearance), and on the account and sign-in page | `src/App.tsx`, `src/AccountLab.tsx` |
| Interface text | All screens: main app (287 texts), account pages, usage, billing, support, vault, library, photo to text, warnings, mode labels (Auto / Schnell / Gründlich), search choice, masking notes, dates and numbers in German format | `src/text/*.ts` and the four rules and search modules |
| Server messages | 108 fixed server messages and 1 template shown in German in the browser; a test fails when a new server message has no German entry | `src/server-messages.ts`, `tests/i18n-server-messages.test.ts` |
| Legal texts | German drafts of the privacy policy (each provider), privacy statement, health consent, provider disclosure, imprint, acceptable use and the 18+ rule | `shared/*.ts`, `docs/*-de-draft-20261113.md` |
| Change-pair check | Recorded hashes of each English and German legal text; a change to either fails the test until the other language is checked and both are re-recorded | `shared/legal-text-pairs.json`, `tests/legal-text-pairs.test.ts` |
| Answer language | The model replies in the language of your own sentences; code, numbers, names and quoted text do not count; with none, it uses the interface language | `src/conversation.ts` |
| German routing and detection | German cues for urgent, code, maths, rewrite, reasoning, time-sensitive and German-rules questions | `src/answer-modes.ts`, `src/german-rules.ts`, `src/when-to-search.ts` |

German text uses the formal "Sie" and the fixed terms in `docs/i18n-glossary-20261113.md`.

## How it was checked

- **Browsers, in German:** the UI sweep in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit (Safari's engine) at phone (with touch), tablet and desktop width, six screens each. **54 of 54 clean:** no English left on screen, no overflow, contrast, focus and touch targets all fine. The English sweep was also 54 of 54 after the changes.
  - The first German run found two gaps, both fixed: there was no language switch in Settings or on the account page, and one checkbox was smaller than the 24 px touch minimum.
- **Automated tests in German:**
  - the main screen at phone and desktop width;
  - the switch, which is kept after a reload, sets the page language, and is in Settings;
  - 10 other screens at phone width;
  - every server message;
  - the legal pairs;
  - consent in either language.
- **German routing (`docs/german-routing-20261113.md`):**
  - Routing agreement on German prompts went from 60 % to 97.5 % (English 100 %).
  - Rules detection on the frozen set went from 4 of 14 to 14 of 14, with no false alarms. The cues were tuned on that set.
  - **On 20 fresh German prompts written after the change, rules detection is only 5 of 12 (42 %).** The question detector still misses many everyday rules questions, so the answer-side check (unchecked-figures warning) stays the safety net. Its false alarms are unchanged at 0.6 %.
- **Answer language:**
  - Stored answers: 302 of 308 German questions got German answers, and the other 6 were notices or only figures. The 40-question set got 90 of 90. No English question got a German answer.
  - Live check on Privatemode in both interfaces, 8 prompts (`evaluation/answer-language-20261113/`): an English question in the German interface got English, a German question in the English interface got German, and "explain in English" was obeyed.
  - The first live run found that a code-only message in the German interface got an English explanation. The rule was made explicit (code does not set the language). After that, 2 of 2 code-only replies were German. This is a spot check, not a statistic.

## Not covered or still open

- **Real Safari on iPhone and real Android phones:** WebKit and touch emulation are close but not the same. This stays in your device check (T2, live-check list rows in `docs/render-deploy-and-live-checks-20261113.md`).
- **The 30 older end-to-end browser specs** (`npm run test:e2e`) find controls by their English names, so they run in English only. German is covered by the new tests and the sweep above.
- **A native German reader** should skim the interface once. Tone and terms follow the glossary, but no native speaker has read it.
- **Left in English on purpose:**
  - text that goes to the model or into stored answers (it becomes chat history);
  - the "DELETE ACCOUNT" confirmation phrase the server checks (the German label explains it);
  - the default title "New conversation".
- **Fast-mode quality notes from the live check** (not language): one wrong sum ("17 * 23 + 4" answered "391") and one self-contradicting code explanation. Auto sends maths and code to Thinking, so normal use is less exposed.

## Before you deploy German

1. **Approve the German legal texts**, with a legal read. German is binding. The open legal questions are in `docs/legal-texts-de-20261113.md`:
   - Add a sentence saying the German version prevails?
   - "eindeutige Erlaubnis" or the GDPR term "ausdrückliche Einwilligung"? Changing it asks for consent again.
   - The imprint heading under § 5 DDG.
   - The two Privatemode texts disagree: token counts "per account" or "per key".
   - The acceptable-use draft still names Tinfoil.
2. Optional: a native reader for the interface text.
3. Until then, deploy without `VITE_GERMAN_UI` (the default): the app is English only and the DE/EN switch is hidden. **Built 13 November, night:** a build setting, `VITE_GERMAN_UI=1`. Without it the app is English everywhere (browser language, a saved pick and the model's fallback language are ignored) and has no DE/EN switch. Default off in both Dockerfiles; test `tests/german-off.test.ts`. A build setting and not `/api/status`, because the sign-in page shows legal text before any status can be read. To switch on after the legal read: add the Render environment variable `VITE_GERMAN_UI` = `1` and redeploy.

## Does it block the alpha date?

**The code does not block it.** It is merged, tested and green. **What can delay the date** is the legal read of the German texts, because German is binding. That legal read was already on your list (step 10) for the English texts; it now covers both languages, and the side-by-side file `docs/legal-texts-side-by-side.md` is ready for the lawyer.

Spend for this work: about 30 Privatemode requests (roughly 30,000 tokens) for the live language checks. No search.
