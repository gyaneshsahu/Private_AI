# Brookvale Water Cooperative — Annual Report for the Financial Year 2025

## 1. Message from the Board

The financial year 2025 was a year of two halves for the Brookvale Water Cooperative. In the first half we completed the largest pipe replacement programme in the cooperative's history and saw the first clear results of our leak detection work. In the second half we faced the driest summer since records began in our catchment, a supplier insolvency that delayed one of our major projects, and a sharp rise in energy prices for pumping. We are proud that, despite these pressures, the water supply to every connected household remained safe and continuous throughout the year, with no boil-water notices issued.

The cooperative is owned by its members, the households and businesses of Brookvale and the surrounding villages of Edderwick, Marsh End and Low Tolland. At the end of the year we served 41,300 households and 1,870 business connections through a network of 612 kilometres of mains. This report sets out how we used members' money, what we achieved, where we fell short, and what we plan for 2026.

The Board met eleven times during the year. Its most significant decision, taken at the meeting of 18 November 2025 after a public consultation, was to approve a tariff increase of 3.5 per cent, taking effect on 1 March 2026. This is the first change to the tariff since 2021. We explain the reasons in section 6.

## 3. Water Resources and the Summer Drought

Brookvale draws most of its water from the Kessel reservoir in the hills to the north of the town, topped up by two boreholes at Marsh End. In a normal year the reservoir is between 70 and 85 per cent full at the end of August. In 2025, after a dry spring and a June and July with less than a third of the usual rainfall, the reservoir fell to 38 per cent in the third week of August, its lowest level since it was built.

We did not need to impose a hosepipe ban, but we came close. From 4 July to 15 September we ran a voluntary water-saving campaign, "Every Drop Counts", which asked members to avoid watering lawns, to take shorter showers and to report leaks. Daily household demand fell by about 9 per cent during the campaign compared with the same weeks in 2024. We are grateful to every member who took part. We also brought the second Marsh End borehole back into service two months earlier than planned, which added around 1,400 cubic metres per day during the driest weeks.

The drought has made the Board look again at our long-term resilience. A study of a possible interconnection with the neighbouring Fairholme supply area will be commissioned in 2026. No decision has been taken, and any proposal will be brought to members before money is committed.

## 4. Capital Projects

We spent EUR 6.8 million on capital projects in 2025, funded from the cooperative's reserves and a long-term loan from the regional development bank. Three projects made up most of this spending.

### 4.1 Northside main replacement — completed

The Northside main replacement replaced 14 kilometres of cast-iron mains, some of them more than ninety years old, with modern polyethylene pipe. The work began in March 2024 and was completed in June 2025, three weeks ahead of schedule and EUR 0.2 million under its EUR 4.1 million budget. The Northside district had accounted for more than a third of all bursts in the network. Since the new mains came into use, the district has had two bursts, compared with twenty-three in the same period a year earlier.

We know that the works were disruptive, especially on Mill Lane and Station Road, where traffic was diverted for several weeks. We thank residents for their patience and the local schools for helping us plan the work around term dates.

### 4.2 Kessel reservoir roof repair — delayed

The covered treated-water tank at the Kessel reservoir has a concrete roof that needs resealing. The work was due to be completed in the autumn of 2025. In May 2025 our main contractor's specialist sealing supplier became insolvent, and the contractor was unable to source an equivalent material that meets drinking-water standards in time for the summer working window. The repair has therefore been rescheduled to the third quarter of 2026. Inspections carried out in September 2025 confirmed that the existing roof remains watertight and that water quality is not at risk; the tank will be inspected monthly until the repair is done. The delay is expected to add around EUR 90,000 to the project's cost, mostly from price increases for materials.

### 4.3 Smart meter pilot — under way

In October 2025 we started a nine-month pilot of 2,000 smart water meters in the Edderwick and Low Tolland areas. Smart meters send a reading every hour, which lets us spot continuous flows that may indicate a leak on a customer's own pipes, and lets members see their own usage in an online account. Participation was voluntary, and every participating household was told what data the meter collects and who can see it. The meters record volume only; they do not record anything about who is using water or when individual appliances run. At the end of the pilot, the Board will decide whether to offer smart meters more widely. The pilot is costing EUR 410,000, of which EUR 150,000 is covered by a regional innovation grant.

## 5. Customer Service

Complaints fell from 1,140 in 2024 to 860 in 2025, a fall of about a quarter. The largest single category of complaints in both years was billing, followed by low pressure and the reinstatement of roads and pavements after our works. We introduced a new rule in 2025: when we dig up a pavement, it must be reinstated within ten working days, and the site must display a sign with a named contact and a reference number. Complaints about reinstatement fell by almost half after this rule was introduced.

The average time to answer a phone call was 74 seconds, against our target of 90 seconds. We answered 96 per cent of written enquiries within five working days. Our new online account, launched in February 2025, now has 11,200 registered users, who can see their bills, report a problem and set up payment plans without phoning us.

We continued our support scheme for members on low incomes. In 2025, 1,320 households received a reduced tariff under the scheme, at a cost to the cooperative of EUR 186,000. This support is funded from the operating surplus, and the Board has committed to keep it in place after the tariff increase.

## 6. Finances and the 2026 Tariff

Operating revenue rose to EUR 23.6 million, mainly because of new connections in the Brookvale West housing development and slightly higher business consumption. Operating costs rose to EUR 21.9 million. Energy for pumping and treatment cost EUR 2.7 million, EUR 0.5 million more than in 2024, because of higher electricity prices in the first half of the year and the extra pumping needed during the drought. Chemicals cost EUR 0.6 million, roughly unchanged. Staff and contractor costs, maintenance, insurance and administration made up the rest.

The operating surplus was EUR 1.7 million, slightly lower than the EUR 1.8 million of 2024. Under the cooperative's rules, the surplus is not paid out to members; it is reinvested in the network or held in reserve.

The tariff has not changed since 2021. Since then our costs for energy, materials and borrowing have risen considerably, and our reserves have been used to fund the Northside replacement and the Kessel repair. Without a price change, the Board's financial plan shows the cooperative moving into an operating deficit in 2027. After a six-week public consultation, in which 2,340 members responded, the Board approved an increase of 3.5 per cent on both the standing charge and the volume charge, taking effect on 1 March 2026. For a typical household using 90 cubic metres a year, this means an increase of about EUR 1.10 per month.

Two alternative proposals were considered and rejected: a larger increase of 6 per cent, which would have allowed faster pipe replacement, and a two-step increase of 2 per cent in 2026 and 2 per cent in 2027. A majority of consultation responses favoured the single, smaller step.

## 7. Water Quality

We carried out 9,870 tests on water samples taken from the reservoir, the treatment works, the boreholes and customers' taps. All but three tests met the drinking-water standard. The three failures were for iron and manganese at taps in older streets, where sediment in old pipes discoloured the water; in each case the main was flushed, follow-up samples passed, and the affected street is now included in the 2026–2028 replacement plan. There were no failures for bacteria.

## 8. Environment

Pumping and treating water is the cooperative's largest use of energy. In 2025 we installed a 450-kilowatt solar array on the roof and grounds of the treatment works, which supplied about 11 per cent of the works' electricity in its first six months. Less leakage also means less energy: every litre that does not leak is a litre we do not have to treat and pump. We planted 2,600 trees along streams in the Kessel catchment as part of a long-term project to reduce soil run-off into the reservoir.

## 9. Looking Ahead to 2026

Our priorities for 2026 are:

1. Complete the Kessel reservoir roof repair in the third quarter.
2. Finish the smart meter pilot in June and bring the Board's decision on a wider roll-out to the members' meeting in the autumn.
3. Begin the 2026–2028 mains replacement plan, starting with the 6 kilometres of oldest pipe in the town centre.
4. Commission the resilience study on a possible interconnection with the Fairholme supply area.
5. Bring non-revenue water below 11 per cent.

We will report on each of these in next year's annual report.

## 10. Governance

The Board has nine members: six elected by the cooperative's members, two appointed by the councils of Brookvale and Marsh End, and one independent member with financial expertise. Board elections were held at the annual members' meeting on 14 June 2025, at which two members were re-elected and one new member was elected. The Board's audit committee met four times. The cooperative's accounts for 2025 were audited by an external audit firm, which issued an unqualified opinion.
